\section*{\centering Abstract}

This report presents an intelligent Information Retrieval (IR) system developed for the categorized BBC News Archive (2,225 articles across five topics: Business, Entertainment, Politics, Sport, and Tech). Traditional search techniques like TF-IDF and BM25 are fast and excel at exact keyword matching, but they suffer from the \textit{vocabulary mismatch problem} when users search using synonyms. On the other hand, modern neural models like Sentence Transformers understand deep semantic meaning and context, but can sometimes overlook specific keyword constraints.

To leverage the strengths of both approaches, we designed a \textbf{Hybrid Search Pipeline} that combines probabilistic Okapi BM25 with dense Sentence Transformers (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}) using \textbf{Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)}. RRF merges search results based on ranking positions rather than raw scores, avoiding the challenge of calibrating different score ranges.

Additionally, the system includes practical search enhancements:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{WordNet Query Expansion:} Automatically enriches queries with synonyms to reduce vocabulary mismatch.
    \item \textbf{Query-Focused Extractive Summarization:} Generates Google-style featured snippets by highlighting the most relevant sentences in retrieved articles.
    \item \textbf{3D Semantic Knowledge Map:} Uses Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to visualize document clusters and queries in an interactive 3D space.
    \item \textbf{Live News Categorization:} Uses a trained Logistic Regression model (96.63\% accuracy) to categorize and index breaking news articles in real time.
\end{itemize}

Empirical evaluations show that the Hybrid RRF model achieves superior performance with a \textbf{Mean Average Precision (MAP) of 0.912} and an \textbf{nDCG@10 of 0.965}, clearly outperforming standalone keyword and semantic search models.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\textbf{Keywords:} Information Retrieval, Hybrid Search, Okapi BM25, Sentence Transformers, Reciprocal Rank Fusion, BBC News, Query Expansion, Extractive Summaries.

\section{Introduction}

Information Retrieval (IR) is the foundational technology that powers search engines, digital news portals, customer support knowledge bases, and modern question-answering systems. At its core, an Information Retrieval system takes an unstructured natural language user query and searches across a large repository of documents to identify, rank, and return the most relevant information within milliseconds.

With the exponential growth of online journalism and digital news media, news archives present unique and demanding search challenges. News articles cover diverse domains such as politics, corporate business, athletics, technology, and arts. Users searching a news repository exhibit diverse search intents: some search for specific named entities (e.g., \textit{``Apple fiscal earnings report''}), while others submit broad conceptual questions (e.g., \textit{``impact of inflation on consumer spending''}). A successful news retrieval engine must satisfy both exact-keyword lookups and abstract conceptual inquiries with equal precision.

Historically, search systems have relied primarily on \textbf{sparse lexical matching algorithms}, such as Term Frequency--Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) and the probabilistic Okapi BM25 algorithm. In these classical formulations, documents and queries are represented as sparse vectors over a fixed vocabulary. An inverted index data structure is constructed to map every vocabulary word to the list of documents containing that word. Lexical methods offer remarkable computational efficiency, low memory footprints, and near-instantaneous query response times.

However, purely lexical matching models face fundamental linguistic limitations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item \textbf{The Vocabulary Mismatch Problem:} Natural language allows the same concept to be expressed in multiple ways. A user querying \textit{``automobile shares slump''} will fail to match a relevant news article titled \textit{``car stock market drops''} under strict keyword matching because the authors used different synonyms.
    \item \textbf{Lack of Semantic Context:} Lexical models operate under the \textit{bag-of-words} assumption, treating words as isolated tokens and ignoring syntax, polysemy (words with multiple meanings), and semantic relationships.
    \item \textbf{Term Saturation Sensitivity:} Simple frequency counters can be skewed by keyword repetition, necessitating careful term frequency damping.
\end{enumerate}

In recent years, the development of deep bidirectional transformer models (such as BERT and Sentence Transformers) has enabled \textbf{dense semantic retrieval}. Instead of matching exact characters or stems, dense encoders convert passages and queries into high-dimensional continuous vector embeddings (e.g., 384 dimensions) where geometric proximity directly corresponds to semantic relatedness. While dense models excel at understanding context, intent, and synonyms, they are computationally intensive and can occasionally drift away from exact keywords or specialized names.

\subsection{Project Objectives}
The primary objective of this project is to build and evaluate an end-to-end \textbf{Hybrid Search Engine} for the categorized BBC News Archive. Specifically, we aim to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Combine sparse lexical retrieval (Okapi BM25) and dense semantic retrieval (Sentence Transformers) using **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)**.
    \item Eliminate the vocabulary mismatch problem using automated **WordNet Query Expansion**.
    \item Provide instant query-focused extractive summaries to act as **Featured Snippets**.
    \item Offer explainability through an interactive **3D PCA Semantic Universe**.
    \item Enable live breaking news categorization and indexing using a trained **Logistic Regression** model.
    \item Measure retrieval quality using industry-standard metrics: Precision@10, Recall, Mean Average Precision (MAP), and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG@10).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dual-Stream Preprocessing Architecture}
A critical design decision in our system is that lexical search and neural transformers require fundamentally different text preparation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Lexical Preprocessing (for BM25 and TF-IDF):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Text is converted to lowercase and punctuation is stripped.
        \item Non-informative grammatical stop words (e.g., \textit{``the''}, \textit{``is''}, \textit{``at''}) are removed using NLTK's English stopword corpus.
        \item Words are reduced to their root forms using the \textbf{Porter Stemmer} (e.g., \textit{``investments''} becomes \textit{``invest''}, \textit{``shares''} becomes \textit{``share''}).
        \item An **Inverted Index** is constructed, mapping each unique stemmed vocabulary term to its posting list containing document IDs and term frequencies.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Semantic Preprocessing (for Sentence Transformers):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item In contrast, the Sentence Transformer is provided with \textbf{raw, grammatically intact sentences}.
        \item Deep neural networks rely on multi-head self-attention mechanisms where capitalization, punctuation, and grammatical stop words provide essential syntactic cues. Removing them degrades embedding quality.
        \item Articles are truncated to a 300-word context window to capture the journalistic lead while conforming to transformer token limits.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Retrieval Models}
We implement and benchmark four distinct ranking strategies:

\subsubsection{1. TF-IDF Vector Space Model (Baseline)}
The Term Frequency--Inverse Document Frequency model represents each document and query as sparse vectors. Relevance is computed using Cosine Similarity:
\begin{equation}
\text{Sim}_{\text{TF-IDF}}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{d}) = \frac{\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{d}}{\|\mathbf{q}\| \|\mathbf{d}\|}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{2. Okapi BM25 Probabilistic Model}
Okapi BM25 improves on TF-IDF by introducing non-linear term saturation (capping the score benefit of repeating terms) and document length normalization:
\begin{equation}
\text{Score}_{\text{BM25}}(D, Q) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{IDF}(q_i) \cdot \frac{f(q_i, D) \cdot (k_1 + 1)}{f(q_i, D) + k_1 \cdot \left(1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)}
\end{equation}
where $k_1 = 1.5$ controls term saturation and $b = 0.75$ controls document length normalization relative to the average length $\text{avgdl}$.

\subsubsection{3. Sentence Transformers (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2})}
We use the pre-trained \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} model, which maps sentences into a continuous 384-dimensional dense vector space $\mathbb{R}^{384}$. Relevance between query vector $\mathbf{u}$ and document vector $\mathbf{v}$ is evaluated using Cosine Similarity:
\begin{equation}
\cos(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\|}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{4. Hybrid Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)}
A key challenge in combining search engines is that BM25 outputs unbounded scores ($[0, \infty)$) while Cosine Similarity outputs normalized scores ($[-1, 1]$). Directly adding these scores causes BM25 to dominate. 

Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) overcomes this by relying entirely on the **ordinal rank position** of documents:
\begin{equation}
\text{RRF\_Score}(d) = \sum_{m \in \{\text{BM25}, \text{Dense}\}} \frac{1}{60 + \text{Rank}_m(d)}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Rank}_m(d)$ is the 1-based position of document $d$ in ranking list $m$, and $k = 60$ is the standard smoothing constant (Cormack et al., 2009). Documents that rank highly across \textit{both} models receive the highest combined score.

\subsection{Key Features Beyond Basic Search}
To enhance practical search utility, the system integrates four specialized features:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Automated WordNet Query Expansion:} The system queries Princeton's WordNet lexicon to automatically identify synonyms for query terms, expanding the search query behind the scenes to address vocabulary mismatch.
    \item \textbf{Query-Focused Extractive Summarization (Featured Snippets):} When an article is retrieved, the system tokenizes it into sentences and scores each sentence based on its token overlap with the query. The top 1--2 sentences are displayed as an instant answer snippet (100\% factual with zero hallucination).
    \item \textbf{3D Semantic Map (Explainable AI):} Using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), the 384-dimensional document vectors are compressed into 3D coordinates ($X, Y, Z$) and rendered as an interactive 3D universe in Plotly. This allows users to visually inspect document clusters and query proximity.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time ML News Categorization:} The system trains a Logistic Regression classifier on the TF-IDF feature space (achieving 96.63\% accuracy). Users can paste breaking news into the interface; the model predicts its category and dynamically indexes it into the search engine in real time.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Results}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We evaluate search performance using four standard Information Retrieval metrics:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Precision@10 (P@10):} The proportion of the top 10 retrieved articles that belong to the relevant topic category:
    \begin{equation}
    P@10 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{10} \text{rel}_i}{10}
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{rel}_i \in \{0, 1\}$ indicates whether document $i$ is relevant.

    \item \textbf{Recall:} The fraction of all relevant documents in the corpus that were retrieved:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Recall} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K} \text{rel}_i}{|R|}
    \end{equation}
    where $|R|$ is the total number of relevant documents for that category in the entire corpus.

    \item \textbf{Mean Average Precision (MAP):} Evaluates the quality of ranking by computing average precision across all retrieved positions, with the denominator bounded by the retrieval window $K$:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{AP@K} = \frac{1}{\min(K, |R|)} \sum_{k=1}^{K} P(k) \times \text{rel}_k
    \end{equation}
    MAP is the mean AP across all evaluation queries: $\text{MAP} = \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{q \in Q} \text{AP}(q)$.

    \item \textbf{Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG@10):} Measures ranking quality by applying a logarithmic discount to documents that appear lower in the list:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{DCG@10} = \sum_{i=1}^{10} \frac{2^{\text{rel}_i} - 1}{\log_2(i + 1)}, \quad \text{nDCG@10} = \frac{\text{DCG@10}}{\text{IDCG@10}}
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{IDCG@10}$ is the Ideal DCG score achieved by placing all relevant documents at the very top.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discussion of Findings}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Why BM25 Outperforms TF-IDF:} Okapi BM25 improves Precision@10 from 0.420 to 0.610 (+45.2\%) and MAP from 0.384 to 0.530 (+38.0\%). This gain is driven by BM25's term saturation factor, which prevents documents that repeat keywords from dominating, and document length normalization, which prevents bias toward longer articles.
    \item \textbf{Why Sentence Transformers Outperform Lexical Models:} Sentence Transformers achieve a MAP of 0.795 and an nDCG@10 of 0.884. By mapping queries and articles into continuous vector space, the neural model recognizes conceptual synonyms (e.g., matching \textit{``economic downturn''} with \textit{``recession''}) that keyword models miss.
    \item \textbf{Why Hybrid RRF Wins:} The Hybrid RRF model achieves the highest overall scores across every metric, reaching **0.940 Precision@10**, **0.912 MAP**, and **0.965 nDCG@10**. By combining the exact-keyword precision of BM25 with the conceptual understanding of Sentence Transformers, RRF ensures that documents supported by both models are prioritized at the top.
    \item \textbf{Note on Recall Values:} While Precision, MAP, and nDCG are high, Recall appears numerically modest ($\approx 0.025$). This is mathematically expected: because each category contains $\approx 400$ relevant articles, retrieving only $K=10$ documents imposes a theoretical recall ceiling of $10 / 400 = 0.025$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

In this project, we designed, implemented, and evaluated an advanced \textbf{Hybrid Information Retrieval System} for the BBC News Archive. 

The experimental results validate that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Purely lexical search (TF-IDF and BM25) is fast but limited by the vocabulary mismatch problem.
    \item Purely semantic search (Sentence Transformers) understands context and intent but can occasionally overlook exact keyword constraints.
    \item \textbf{Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)} provides the optimal combination, achieving an exceptional **0.965 nDCG@10** and **0.912 MAP**, outperforming all standalone search baselines.
\end{enumerate}

Furthermore, the integration of automated WordNet query expansion, query-focused extractive snippets, 3D semantic mapping, and real-time news categorization creates an accurate, explainable, and production-ready search engine.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Scope}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Compute Overhead:} Generating dense transformer embeddings on CPU requires noticeable time during initialization. In a production deployment with millions of documents, specialized Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) vector databases (such as Faiss, Milvus, or Pinecone) would be used.
    \item \textbf{Extractive vs. Generative Answers:} Our snippet generator is strictly extractive to eliminate AI hallucinations. Future work could integrate lightweight generative re-rankers (such as MonoT5) to synthesize answers across multiple paragraphs.
\end{itemize}
